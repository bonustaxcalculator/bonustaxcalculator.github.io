⚙️ The Process

How Bonus Tax Withholding Works

The IRS treats bonuses as "supplemental wages." Here's exactly how the math works — step by step.

01
📄

Identify Supplemental Wages

Bonuses, commissions, overtime, severance, and tips are all classified as supplemental wages by the IRS — separate from your regular salary.

02
🏛️

Federal Withholding Applied

Employers choose between the 22% flat supplemental rate or the aggregate method. Bonuses over $1 million are withheld at 37%.

03
🗺️

State Tax Deducted

Most states also withhold income tax from bonuses. Rates range from 0% (TX, FL, NV) to over 13% (CA). Our calculator uses each state's actual withholding rate.

04
🔒

FICA Taxes Collected

Social Security (6.2% up to $168,600 wage base) and Medicare (1.45%, plus 0.9% surtax above $200K) are withheld just like on regular wages.

05
📊

True Liability at Filing

Withholding is an estimate. Your actual tax on the bonus is determined at year-end when your total income is calculated on your Form 1040.

06
💡

Possible Refund or Owe

If the flat 22% rate is higher than your marginal bracket, you may receive a refund. If it's lower (rare), you may owe more at filing.

⚖️ Comparison

Flat Rate vs. Aggregate Method

Two IRS-approved methods for withholding taxes on bonuses — here's the difference and when each applies.

Most Common
⚡

Flat Rate Method

22% Federal

  • ✅ Simple, predictable withholding
  • ✅ No need to know annual income
  • ✅ Applied to first $1 million in supplemental wages
  • ⚠️ 37% rate kicks in above $1 million
  • ℹ️ May over-withhold if your bracket is below 22%
Formula: Bonus × 22% = Federal Withholding
Precise
🎯

Aggregate Method

Based on Your Bracket

  • ✅ More accurate to actual tax liability
  • ✅ Considers regular wages + bonus combined
  • ✅ Better for high earners in lower brackets
  • ⚠️ More complex calculation
  • ℹ️ Employer applies annualized pay + bonus tax
Formula: Combined tax – Regular wage tax = Withholding
💡

Pro Tip: High earners in the 12–22% bracket may prefer the aggregate method to avoid over-withholding. If your marginal rate is above 22%, the flat rate actually under-withholds, meaning you may owe at filing.

💡 Smart Strategies

Ways to Reduce Bonus Taxes

You can't avoid taxes, but you can be smart about minimizing them legally.

🏦

Maximize 401(k) Contributions

If you receive a large bonus, consider asking your employer to apply it toward your 401(k) up to the annual limit ($23,000 in 2024, $30,500 if 50+). Pre-tax contributions reduce your taxable bonus dollar-for-dollar.

Saves: Up to 37% on contributed amount
🏥

Fund Your HSA or FSA

If eligible, contribute to a Health Savings Account (HSA: $4,150 single / $8,300 family in 2024) or Flexible Spending Account before your bonus is paid to lower taxable income.

Saves: Federal + state + FICA taxes
📅

Time Your Bonus Wisely

If you're near a tax bracket threshold, having your bonus paid in January instead of December (or vice versa) can shift income into a lower-tax year — especially if you expect income changes.

Saves: Varies by bracket difference
🎁

Charitable Donations

Donating to a qualified charity in the same tax year as your bonus can offset income. A donor-advised fund lets you front-load multiple years of donations in one lump sum for maximum deduction.

Saves: Up to your marginal tax rate
🏠

Defer to Traditional IRA

Contributing to a Traditional IRA (up to $7,000, or $8,000 if 50+) may be deductible depending on your income and employer retirement plan coverage, further reducing your taxable income.

Saves: Based on eligibility & bracket
📋

Adjust Your W-4

Updating your W-4 withholding elections — especially adding extra withholding in the period you receive your bonus — can prevent a surprise tax bill at year-end without changing your overall take-home dramatically.

Benefit: Avoid underpayment penalties
